About

Research focused on physical activity measurement and promotion in low-resource settings

I am a public health researcher specialising in physical activity epidemiology, with particular interests in accelerometry and wearable devices, physical activity measurement and surveillance, and community-based interventions in low- and middle-income countries.

I completed my PhD at the Department of Public Health, Aarhus University, Denmark, in 2026. During my PhD, I led research on the measurement and promotion of physical activity among adults in Nepal, combining epidemiological research, device-based physical activity measurement, intervention development and evaluation through a cluster-randomised controlled trial.

My current research interests centre on improving physical activity measurement in resource-constrained settings, integrating wearable devices into population physical activity surveillance, and developing scalable community- and health-system-based approaches to physical activity promotion and non-communicable disease prevention.

Research

Core research areas

Accelerometry & wearables

Device-based assessment of physical activity and sedentary behaviour using wrist-worn accelerometers, with emphasis on measurement validity and interpretation.

Measurement & surveillance

Comparing device-measured and self-reported physical activity and examining how measurement choices shape population-level estimates and surveillance.

Intervention science

Developing and evaluating community-based physical activity interventions, including delivery through Nepal's Female Community Health Volunteer system.

LMIC physical activity research

Strengthening physical activity epidemiology, surveillance and scalable NCD prevention approaches in Nepal and other low- and middle-income countries.

Featured programme

COBIN-PA

Community-Based Promotion of Physical Activity in Nepal

Role: Project Lead / Principal Investigator

COBIN-PA is a research programme examining the measurement and promotion of physical activity among adults in Nepal. The programme combines wrist-worn accelerometry, self-reported physical activity assessment, epidemiological analysis, intervention development and a cluster-randomised controlled trial.

The intervention was delivered through Female Community Health Volunteers and evaluated using objectively measured physical activity outcomes. The wider programme also addresses methodological questions relevant to physical activity measurement and surveillance in low- and middle-income settings.
